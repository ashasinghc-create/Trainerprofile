01 · Profile

From teaching technology to developing people

My journey as a trainer began before I formally held the title of a trainer. At Hutchison Essar, as the SPOC for UMS and Roaming Services, I conducted teach-back sessions to help colleagues understand how the technology worked. That taught me a lesson I still carry: knowing something is very different from being able to help someone else understand it.

My career moved through independent training assignments, quality and operations roles, and into a formal Capability Development Trainer role at IBM, where I designed and delivered programmes in communication, language, accent neutralisation, professional and email etiquette, and customer experience.

Training did not stop when I moved out of a trainer designation. It continued through my roles in PMO, change management, workload management, operations and people leadership. Across these roles I became increasingly interested in why people struggle to apply what they know, what prevents them from progressing, and how organisations can support meaningful capability development.

Today I bring together my corporate experience and training background to create practical learning experiences that connect personal development with workplace application.

03 · Our Development Philosophy

The 5A Framework — inside-out development

Sustainable development begins with awareness and continues through practice, reflection and action. Before adding another skill, framework or behaviour, we need to understand the foundation that is already there.

STEP 1
AWARE
Where am I?

Understand current strengths, behaviours and development needs — what you do well, what limits you, and how others perceive you.

STEP 2
AWAKEN
Where do I want to go?

Recognise new possibilities and why change matters. Get clear on the destination, what it requires, and the gap between today and tomorrow.

STEP 3
APPLY
What do I need for the journey?

Practise new skills and behaviours in relevant situations. Close the gap through learning, practice, exposure and real work application.

STEP 4
ASSESS
Am I actually moving?

Reflect on progress, review application and identify further gaps. Measure behaviour and results, then recalibrate.

STEP 5
ASCEND
Have I reached the next stage?

Build on learning and prepare for the next stage. Make change sustainable — every destination becomes a new starting point.

NOT A 180° TRANSFORMATION. A SMARTER REBUILD.

The objective is not to change everything about a person. It is to understand what already works, identify what is missing and determine what needs to be:

ADDEDSTRENGTHENEDADAPTEDREMOVEDREARRANGED
04 · Our Approach to Organisational Learning

Start with the problem, not a catalogue

Not every workplace problem can be solved by delivering a training session. Our approach begins by understanding the problem before recommending an intervention.

1
IDENTIFY

Understand the workplace challenge and the desired outcome. What is actually happening?

2
DIAGNOSE

Why is it happening? Is the gap knowledge, skill, behaviour, mindset, process, leadership or communication?

3
DESIGN

Develop a suitable learning intervention based on the identified need.

4
DELIVER

Facilitate practical learning through discussion, exercises, workplace scenarios and application.

5
REASSESS

Review learning, application and progress. What was applied, what worked, what still needs attention?

Where appropriate, this can include Training Needs Analysis, customised learning design, participant workbooks and post-programme evaluation. The aim is to make learning relevant to the workplace rather than treating training as an isolated event.

The Glass Box

Watching the career ladder from inside a glass box.

The opportunities are visible. The next role is visible. Others are progressing. Yet something prevents people from moving: a lack of self-awareness, an unclear picture of what the next level requires, or gaps that surface after the promotion.

Career progression is not simply about being given an opportunity. It is about being aware enough to recognise it, prepared enough to take it, and open enough to continue learning once you get there.
